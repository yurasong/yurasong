## About me

I am a **bioinformatician and postdoctoral researcher** in the **Laboratory of Stem Cells and Cancer** at the **Université libre de Bruxelles**, working with Prof. Cédric Blanpain.

My research focuses on how epithelial cells establish their identity, change state and interact with their surroundings during **development, homeostasis, regeneration and tumour initiation**. I combine transcriptomics, chromatin accessibility and spatial information to investigate these processes in glandular epithelia and skin.

With a background in both **molecular biology and bioinformatics**, I approach computational analysis through the biological question. I am particularly interested in connecting gene regulatory networks and cell–cell communication with experimentally observed changes in cell behaviour.

> **Research focus:** epithelial plasticity, gene regulation and tissue organisation through single-cell, spatial and multi-omics analysis.

## Selected projects

### 🧬 [Prostate stem cell plasticity & tumour initiation](https://github.com/yurasong/Pten-Code_repository-Nat_Gen-2025)

Analysis code accompanying our study of **innate immunity and NF-κB signalling in prostate stem cell plasticity, reprogramming and tumour initiation**. Includes single-cell transcriptomics, trajectory analysis, gene regulatory network inference, and bulk RNA-seq and ATAC-seq workflows.

📄 [Jiang, Song et al. — *Nature Cancer* (2025)](https://www.nature.com/articles/s43018-025-00994-3)

### 🔗 [Pik3ca-driven mammary epithelial plasticity](https://github.com/yurasong/Pik3ca_MG_analysis)

Single-cell transcriptomic and multiome analyses investigating **mammary epithelial plasticity**, including regulatory network inference with pySCENIC and SCENIC+.

## How I work

I start with a biological question, choose analyses that address it, and interpret the results alongside the experimental evidence. My aim is to connect molecular changes with cell behaviour and tissue organisation, while keeping the distinction between an observation, a predicted interaction and a mechanism clear.

I value transparent analysis choices, reproducible code and figures that make complex results easier to discuss with experimental collaborators.
